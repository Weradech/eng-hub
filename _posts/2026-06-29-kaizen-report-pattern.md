> **TL;DR** — State the problem, the verified current state, the change, the measured effect and the next owner action. Keep observations, proposed work and approved decisions separate.

![Five sections of a Kaizen report: problem, current state, change, measurement and next action](/assets/img/2026-06-29/kaizen-report-pattern.svg)

This is a report layout. The earlier version used specific inventory quantities, currency values, dates, staff labels and a before/after chart without a linked source dataset. Those figures were illustrative and have been removed so they cannot be mistaken for a company KPI or proven improvement.

## 1. Problem and scope

Name the process, product or population, period, symptom and decision needed. If a loss or defect count is unknown, leave it unknown. Record who reported the issue and which evidence was inspected. Avoid describing a suspected cause as if it were already verified.

## 2. Current state and baseline

Define the measurement, numerator, denominator, source system, query or method, extraction time and exclusions. A baseline from a previous report may be valid if its scope and data are still traceable; a live dashboard is not automatically more accurate. Reconcile data across source systems before putting one number on a chart.

## 3. Change or proposed countermeasure

Describe exactly what changed, where, when and for which population. If the action is only proposed, label it **PROPOSED** and list required approval. Capture a rollback or containment method when the process can affect stock, quality or customers. Link the implementation evidence rather than relying on a narrative of completion.

## 4. Effectiveness check

Compare before and after using the same definitions, or explain the difference. Show the observation window and sample size. Consider confounders such as a different product mix, supplier, fixture, staffing or season. A drop in an alert count may mean the alert was disabled; it does not by itself prove fewer defects. If the post-change period is too short, mark the result **PENDING** and name the closure trigger.

## 5. Next action and decision

| Field | Fill with actual evidence |
|---|---|
| Owner | Named responsible role or person. |
| Action | Specific containment, verification or standardization step. |
| Due date | Agreed date, or visibly pending if not agreed. |
| Decision | Approved scope, restrictions and actual authority. |
| Closure | Evidence that the action worked and remaining open issues. |

A concise report may use one chart or none. Choose the presentation that makes the measurement and decision clear. Do not fill missing KPI results, approvals or dates to make the report look complete. For a stock-data incident, the [reconciliation method]({% post_url 2026-06-13-reconciliation-without-false-alarms %}) helps define a valid comparison before claiming improvement.
